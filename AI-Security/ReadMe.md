# AI Security Portfolio

## Overview

This section of the **Cybersecurity-IT-Portfolio** demonstrates the practical application of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Generative AI to cybersecurity and IT security workflows.

The portfolio focuses on using AI as a **security productivity, analysis, research, and automation capability** while maintaining human oversight, security validation, data protection, and organizational policy requirements.

Projects are built around realistic cybersecurity scenarios rather than isolated AI demonstrations.

---

## Portfolio Objectives

The AI-Security portfolio is designed to demonstrate the ability to:

* Apply Generative AI to practical cybersecurity tasks.
* Develop effective, context-aware prompts.
* Use AI to assist with security research and analysis.
* Apply AI to IAM, SOC, security operations, automation, and data analysis workflows.
* Evaluate the accuracy and limitations of AI-generated information.
* Validate security claims against authoritative sources.
* Identify hallucinations, unsupported assumptions, and overly broad claims.
* Use AI responsibly when handling security-related information.
* Apply secure data-handling practices when working with AI tools.
* Combine AI capabilities with cybersecurity knowledge and human judgment.
* Progress from foundational AI skills toward practical AI-assisted security engineering and automation.

# Lab Documentation & Evidence

Each completed lab is self-contained.

Every lab includes:

```text
Lab-XX-Name/
│
├── README.md
│
└── Screenshots/
    ├── 01-...
    ├── 02-...
    ├── 03-...
    └── ...
```

The lab's `README.md` contains the documentation, while its `Screenshots/` folder contains the evidence supporting the work.

### Evidence Principles

Evidence will:

* Demonstrate meaningful hands-on work.
* Be limited to the screenshots necessary to prove the activity.
* Use consistent naming conventions.
* Be directly linked from the corresponding lab README.
* Avoid exposing sensitive, confidential, or personally identifiable information.

The goal is to demonstrate **credible evidence without unnecessary screenshots**.

# Responsible AI & Security

AI tools can introduce security, privacy, confidentiality, intellectual-property, and compliance risks when used improperly.

This portfolio follows the following principles.

## Protect Sensitive Information

Sensitive organizational information should not be submitted to an AI service unless the organization has explicitly authorized the tool and intended use case.

Examples include:

* Passwords and authentication secrets
* API keys and access tokens
* Personally identifiable information (PII)
* Customer or employee records
* Confidential business information
* Security incident information
* Sensitive network architecture
* Proprietary source code
* Regulated or contractually restricted information

## Follow Organizational Policies

Before using AI for work-related activities, verify applicable:

* Acceptable Use Policies
* Generative AI policies
* Data Classification Policies
* Data Loss Prevention requirements
* Privacy requirements
* Regulatory requirements
* Approved AI platforms and services
* Third-party data-sharing requirements

When requirements are unclear, consult the appropriate security, privacy, compliance, legal, or management team.

## Validate AI Output

AI-generated information should not automatically be treated as authoritative.

Important cybersecurity claims should be evaluated against appropriate authoritative sources, such as:

* CISA
* NIST
* Microsoft security documentation
* Vendor security documentation
* Applicable standards and frameworks
* Organizational policies and procedures

## Maintain Human Oversight

AI may assist with research, analysis, documentation, and automation, but security decisions remain subject to human review, organizational requirements, and professional judgment.

> **AI should augment cybersecurity expertise — not replace it.**

# Portfolio Philosophy

This portfolio prioritizes **quality, evidence, and practical application over project volume**.

Each completed lab is intended to demonstrate:

1. A realistic security problem.
2. A practical AI application.
3. Hands-on work.
4. Human analysis.
5. Security validation.
6. Evidence of the work performed.
7. A documented outcome.

The objective is to demonstrate that AI is being used **responsibly and effectively within cybersecurity workflows**, rather than simply demonstrating familiarity with an AI chatbot.
